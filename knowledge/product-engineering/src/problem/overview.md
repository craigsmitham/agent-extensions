# Product decisions and accountability

Deciding which problems are worth solving is a responsibility in its own right,
distinct from producing requirements or maximizing feature throughput. It means
enabling sound decisions about which problems matter and whether proposed and
delivered solutions achieve the intended
outcomes.[^svpg-product-management]

The concepts in this section form a connected loop rather than a
sequence:[^svpg-product-model]

1. Strategy supplies consequential choices and constraints.
2. Evidence about offerings, audiences, needs, jobs, and value propositions
   gives those choices product meaning.[^product-value-demand]
3. Teams receive problems and outcomes rather than predetermined solutions.
4. Discovery reduces the important product risks with evidence.
5. Real use supplies evidence for the next decision.

Where the arena itself is chosen belongs to
[Where to play](../strategy/). Committing to a solution concept, constructing
it, shipping it, and operating it belong to the sections that follow this one.

## What this section does not own

This section does not own general strategy theory, every decision a product
team makes, project coordination, engineering delivery, design execution,
sales, or marketing. It sits alongside those disciplines while retaining
particular accountability for product value and viability.

It is also not an organizational prescription. Titles and structures vary; the
enduring questions are where product decisions are made, which evidence informs
them, and who is accountable for the resulting value.

# What to solve

Which problems are worth solving, and what outcome would tell us we succeeded?

Value, demand, and the risks that decide whether a candidate is worth pursuing
at all. This section owns the problem and the outcome, and stays in the problem
space: it names what to solve, not how. Choosing a solution concept and
committing to it belongs to [What to build](../solution/); the arena the
problem sits inside belongs to [Where to play](../strategy/).

A problem here may be stated as an unmet need, a job someone is trying to make
progress on, or an explicit problem statement. All three name a gap worth
closing, and none of them presumes a solution.

## Value and demand

A product decision needs a shared account of what is offered, for whom, what
progress or problem matters, and why the offering should help. These concepts
are portable: they assume no particular tracker, document form, delivery
method, or stage model, and the rest of this section uses them rather than
redefining them.

## Teams and cadence

These two concepts describe who does the choosing and at what rhythm, rather
than a problem, an outcome, or the evidence for one. They are kept here because
this section's question has a subject and a tempo: someone must hold the
authority to decide which problems are worth solving, and evidence that an
outcome occurred arrives over a cadence rather than at a moment. [Product
decisions and accountability](overview.md) states that dependency directly.

Their home is unsettled, and this index says so rather than implying otherwise.
Neither concept answers "which problems are worth solving, and what outcome
would tell us we succeeded?" on its own terms, and both would read as naturally
in a section about how a product organization is arranged, which this bundle
does not have. They stay here until that placement question is settled; read the
grouping as an open question, not as a claim that team design belongs to the
problem space.

  complementary expertise, and accountability for outcomes.

## Boundaries

Usability appears here as a risk to be retired by evidence. The craft of
designing for it belongs to [What to build](../solution/).

Discovery and delivery are named here as the two kinds of work a product team
joins. The mechanics of moving a change toward release belong to
[How to ship it](../delivery/), and keeping the released product healthy
belongs to [How to run it](../operations/).

Where product meaning ends and a normative requirement begins is explained in
[Product meaning and requirements](../solution/product-meaning-and-requirements.md),
which sits with the section that owns the requirement.

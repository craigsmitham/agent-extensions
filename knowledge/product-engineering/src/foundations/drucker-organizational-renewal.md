# Drucker's four disciplines of organizational renewal

How does an institution sustain today's performance while creating tomorrow's?
Peter Drucker argued that every institution needs four continuing activities
within ordinary management. Businesses, universities, hospitals, and
public-service organizations all need to release resources, improve performance,
build on success, and create new possibilities. These responsibilities run in
parallel.[^innovative-culture]

| Discipline | What it contributes |
| --- | --- |
| **Organized abandonment** | Regularly reconsiders commitments whose future contribution no longer justifies their resources. |
| **Continuous improvement** | Systematically improves the quality, effectiveness, and efficiency of existing work. |
| **Exploiting success** | Investigates what is working unusually well and commits attention and resources to extending it. |
| **Systematic innovation** | Deliberately develops different offerings and approaches that can replace today's successful ones. |

The four activities distinguish decisions about stopping, improving, extending,
and creating.[^innovative-culture] In this reading, treating them as parallel
responsibilities keeps renewal from becoming a sequence in which innovation
waits until all existing work has been perfected.

Abandonment makes room for the others: capable people occupied with maintaining
yesterday are unavailable to create tomorrow. Its underlying question is whether
we would make the same commitment with what we know today, and whether we would
carry it out in the same way. The answer may call for changing the approach as
well as discontinuing an activity.[^change-leaders]

As an illustrative example, a library might retire an unused service, shorten
reservation waiting times, extend a successful literacy program to another
branch, and develop a new way to serve homebound readers. Improving an existing
service and extending a proven program contribute differently from creating a
new offering.

**Piloting connects proposed changes to experience.** Test improvements and new
offerings on a small scale with a committed participant or customer. Research
and modeling can inform the proposal; they cannot substitute for seeing how it
works in use.[^piloting-change]

Within product engineering, this provides a lens for
[participation and investment choices](../strategy/),
[outcomes and evidence](../problem/outcomes-and-evidence.md), and
[care for existing products](../maintenance/). The four responsibilities help
frame those decisions; they leave the allocation of effort to the circumstances.

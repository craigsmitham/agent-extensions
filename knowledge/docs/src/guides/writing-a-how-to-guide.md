# How to write a how-to guide

Start with a request from someone doing the work. If you have a draft
already, find the request it was meant to answer. Keep it beside you as
you write and try the directions.

## 1. Name what the reader wants to finish

“Document booklet printing” leaves you with a whole print dialog to
describe. Ask who needs the directions and how they’ll know the job is
done. Take the volunteer in *How-to guides and the work
they serve*. They have the final PDF for tonight’s concert and know how
to print it. The ushers need folded programs they can give to the
audience.[^volunteer]

Give your draft a title that names that result. *How to print a concert
program as a folded booklet* gives you a first decision about scope.
Begin with the finished PDF open. End when the volunteer can turn the
pages of a program in order. Write down what your own reader brings to
the task and how they will recognize success. If their aim is to
practice a new skill, design a tutorial around that learning goal.[^diataxis][^work]

## 2. Work through the task and capture the decisions

Do the job yourself or watch someone do it. Jot down what you use,
where you pause to decide, and what you inspect before going on. Ask
about any step an experienced hand takes without mentioning it.

For the concert program, try making one copy. A note such as “print
both sides” hides a decision. Does the printer turn the sheets over?
That determines whether the volunteer can print the whole booklet in
one pass or must reload the paper. Check the print settings against
Adobe’s directions. For the manual route, find the reloading
instructions for the printer the volunteer will use.[^booklet]

## 3. Put the choice where the reader can act on it

Arrange your notes in the order the reader can use them. Write the
shared actions once, give each choice a condition the reader can
recognize, and bring the paths together at the next shared action.
For instance, the middle of the volunteer’s guide could read like
this.[^diataxis][^booklet]

> In Acrobat’s print dialog, choose your printer and select **Booklet**.
> Set the number of copies to one for the first program.
>
> - If the printer turns the sheets automatically, choose **Both sides**
>   under **Booklet subset** and print.
> - If you turn the sheets yourself, print **Front side only**. Reload
>   the sheets as your printer directs, then print **Back side only**
>   with the same booklet settings.
>
> Nest the sheets and fold them together. Turn through the program from
> the front cover. Check that every page is upright and in reading order
> before printing the rest.

Decide how much detail the reader needs at each step. If page eight
beside page one makes the volunteer hesitate, add a sentence before
they print. *Check the preview of the outside sheet. The front and back
covers should be side by side.* If readers also ask why Acrobat pairs
the pages that way, give that question its own explanation and link to
it at the preview. The companion guide works through that very
question.[^explainer]

## 4. Try the draft and revise where it leaves someone guessing

Give the draft and the starting materials to someone who does this kind
of work, if one is available. Let them follow it. Notice the first place
they supply a missing instruction or ask for help. If you are working
alone, start again with the draft in front of you and record any action
you have to supply from memory.

Suppose the folded program has upside-down pages after a manual second
pass. Go back to “reload the sheets.” Try the printer’s loading directions
with a fresh copy. Once you get a correctly folded program, replace that
phrase with the verified loading direction for that model, or link to
the exact diagram if the guide serves several printers. Try the revised
passage on one more copy.

Compare the result with the one you named in step 1. For the program,
open the folded copy and read all eight pages. Record which printer and
which route produced it. If you only had a manual printer, identify
Adobe’s instructions as the basis for the automatic route and leave
that branch marked for a trial on a duplex printer.

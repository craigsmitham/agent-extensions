# How-to guides and the work they serve

The eight pages of tonight’s concert program are ready. The volunteer
printing them knows how to print a PDF. The puzzle is how to get eight
pages onto two sheets so that, once folded, the program reads in order.
On the outside of the booklet, page eight belongs beside page one.

A **how-to guide** gives a practitioner a way to accomplish a particular
task. In Diátaxis, the reader comes to a guide with a purpose of their
own and some skill they can put to work. The volunteer can recognize
a correctly folded program and still need directions for producing one.
That gap is a normal part of practicing a craft, even for someone who has
done the job before.[^how-to][^foundations]

## The task gives the guide its shape

Adobe’s *Print booklets* follows the job from an open document in Acrobat
to a stack of paper that someone folds and staples. A description of the
print dialog could account for every control on the screen and end when
the window closes. The volunteer’s question carries on past that point.
Sending a file to the printer counts as success only if the sheets can
become the program the audience will hold.[^booklet]

That purpose also explains where the guide begins. Writing the program
and proofreading the performers’ names are part of preparing for the concert.
By the time the volunteer looks for printing directions, those decisions
are settled. A how-to guide can cover one stretch of a larger undertaking
because the reader knows what came before and what they intend to do
next.[^how-to]

## Why directions leave room for judgment

Two volunteers could start with the same PDF and need different directions.
One printer turns the sheets over automatically. The other needs a second
pass, and the way the paper goes back into the tray depends on the printer.
Adobe identifies the choice and points the reader to the printer’s own
instructions for reloading. The booklet sets the destination. The equipment
in the room determines part of the route.[^booklet]

This is why a decision can be as much a part of a how-to guide as a physical
action. The author knows which difference between printers matters. The
volunteer knows which printer is on the table. Useful directions bring
those two pieces of knowledge together at the moment of choice.[^how-to]

## The same booklet in a lesson

In a printmaking class, a teacher might hand out two blank sheets and ask
students to fold them, number the pages, then unfold them again. Seeing
where page eight lands is the point of the exercise. At the concert,
the volunteer may learn exactly the same thing along the way. The ushers
are still waiting for their programs.

Diátaxis locates the difference between a tutorial and a how-to guide in
those two obligations. The teacher chooses an activity for the experience
it gives the learner. The volunteer chooses a guide for the help it gives
with work already in hand. Months later, that same volunteer might consult
the guide again at a different printer. Remembering every setting was
never the purpose of the evening.[^tutorial]

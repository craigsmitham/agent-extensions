# Lifecycle and subtraction

Durable knowledge creates a maintenance obligation. Capture is justified only
when the meaning matters beyond the immediate task and cannot be recovered
easily and reliably from its natural authority.

A useful lifecycle has four decisions:

- **Admit:** the claim is durable, reusable, appropriately scoped, and has an
  owner.
- **Maintain:** use and new evidence improve the canonical source rather than
  creating parallel copies. This reflects the KCS emphasis on reuse as an
  opportunity to improve knowledge.[^kcs]
- **Deprecate:** readers still need the old identity, but a successor now owns
  the meaning.
- **Remove:** the claim is obsolete, mechanically recoverable, too local, or no
  longer worth human review.

Maintenance should be demand-informed. Actual use, contradictions, failed
retrieval, and changed evidence are stronger signals than ceremonial review
dates. A time-based review marker is useful only when the subject has a real
expiry or someone is committed to act on the date.

Subtraction is part of maintenance, not a cleanup after it. When a better
canonical source is established, remove the old copy in the same change when
possible. If pruning leaves a document with no independently useful meaning,
remove the document rather than preserve its shape.

The objective is not a complete record of everything once known. It is a small,
current network of claims whose authority remains legible.

[^kcs]: KCS treats knowledge as something reused and improved in the flow of
    work rather than captured once and stored unchanged.

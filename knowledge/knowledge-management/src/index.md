# Knowledge management

Portable principles for deciding what knowledge should exist, where its authority
belongs, and how it stays trustworthy. The concepts below are explanations rather
than procedures: they describe the responsibilities and tradeoffs, and leave
tool-specific and repository-specific practice to the guidance that owns it.

## Start here

- [Knowledge management overview](overview.md) — What knowledge management is
  responsible for and how authority, discovery, trust, and lifecycle form one
  system.

## Authority and form

Which source owns a claim, and which medium can sustain it.

- [Authority and ownership](authority-and-ownership.md) — Why a durable claim
  needs one canonical owner and how summaries, pointers, and local applications
  avoid competing definitions.
- [Knowledge forms](knowledge-forms.md) — How prose, executable contracts,
  instructions, evidence, and live systems own different kinds of knowledge.

## Use and maintenance

How a reader reaches a claim, how much reliance it warrants, and how the corpus
stays small and current.

- [Discovery and structure](discovery-and-structure.md) — How routing layers,
  stable concepts, and search metadata make knowledge findable without
  duplicating it at every entry point.
- [Lifecycle and subtraction](lifecycle-and-subtraction.md) — How durable
  knowledge is admitted, maintained, deprecated, and removed so the corpus stays
  trustworthy and small.
- [Provenance and trust](provenance-and-trust.md) — How source, transformation,
  evidence, currency, and review determine what confidence a knowledge consumer
  should place in a claim.

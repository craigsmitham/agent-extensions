# Learning checklists

A learning checklist should help a learner build knowledge, judgment, or
self-regulation that remains when the aid is absent. Successful completion
while the checklist is visible establishes supported performance, not
learning.

## Bind the learning claim

Name:

- what the learner should later recall, explain, decide, or perform;
- current and intended expertise;
- whether the checklist supports acquisition, deliberate practice,
  self-monitoring, reflection, assessment, or transfer;
- what feedback is available and who supplies it;
- when support should fade; and
- the delayed, unassisted situation in which learning should transfer.

If the task requires substantial explanation, modeling, practice, feedback, or
assessment, use a lesson, worked example, simulation, coaching protocol, or
rubric as the primary artifact. A checklist can scaffold parts of that system.

## Design for learning rather than compliance

- Prompt retrieval, prediction, or explanation before revealing an answer when
  that is safe and appropriate.
- Connect important items to their rationale, cues, and consequences instead of
  presenting unexplained commands.
- Include feedback that distinguishes a correct result from a lucky or ritual
  response.
- Prompt reflection on exceptions, errors, and reasoning where judgment is the
  learning target.
- Vary practice conditions when transfer matters; repeating one fixed list can
  overfit performance to the prompt.
- Fade detail, prompts, or frequency as competence grows, while preserving
  independent verification that remains operationally necessary.
- Let experts bypass redundant instructional support without removing critical
  controls merely because they are experienced.

Do not mix learning and summative assessment invisibly. If completion is
scored, specify the evidence and interpretation. A checkbox that records
participation does not establish comprehension or competence.

## Evaluate the learning outcome

Measure at the level claimed:

| Claim | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Aided completion | Performance while the checklist is available |
| Recall or understanding | Delayed recall, explanation, or concept application |
| Independent performance | Delayed unassisted task execution |
| Transfer | Performance in materially different cases or environments |
| Self-regulation | Planning, monitoring, correction, and reflection without equivalent prompts |

Compare persistent, faded, and absent support when deskilling or dependency is
plausible. Track time, errors, confidence calibration, help-seeking, and
out-of-scope cases as well as completion.

Disconfirm a learning claim when performance collapses without the checklist,
learners cannot explain or adapt the steps, experts are hindered by redundant
support, or a feasible practice-and-feedback method produces better retention
or transfer.

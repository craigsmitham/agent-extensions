# Advantage and coherence

Advantage is the reason an organization's chosen way of participating is
expected to produce better results than credible alternatives. It may come from
distinctive customer value, lower resource requirements, privileged access,
network effects, accumulated capability, or another mechanism suited to the
context.

A strength is not automatically an advantage. It matters strategically only
when it improves the chosen way to win and is difficult for alternatives to
neutralize. Likewise, differentiation is not valuable merely because it is
different; it must change behavior or economics that matter.

Coherence makes advantage systemic. Where-to-play choices focus capability
investment, capabilities enable the chosen way to win, and management systems
reinforce those capabilities. Competitors may copy an isolated feature or
practice more easily than an integrated set of mutually reinforcing choices.

Coherence does not mean permanence. A tightly fitted strategy can become
brittle when its environment changes. The organization should know which
assumptions support the fit and which signals would require a different system
of choices.

Evaluating one strategic choice in isolation misses the main question: whether
the complete system creates distinctive value in the context actually chosen.

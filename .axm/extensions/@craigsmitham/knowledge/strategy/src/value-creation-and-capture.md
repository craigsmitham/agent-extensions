# Value creation and capture

The value stick separates four quantities in an exchange:

1. **Willingness to pay (WTP):** the most a customer would pay.
2. **Price:** what the customer actually pays.
3. **Cost:** what the organization incurs to provide the offering.
4. **Willingness to sell (WTS):** the least compensation a supplier or employee
   would accept to contribute.

Their differences distinguish created value from captured value:

- Customer value is WTP minus price.
- Organizational margin is price minus cost.
- Supplier or employee value is cost minus WTS.
- Total value created is WTP minus WTS.

Price redistributes value between the customer and organization but does not by
itself increase total value. Raising WTP through a better customer experience or
lowering WTS by making participation more attractive expands the total available
value. Lowering cost without lowering WTS can increase organizational capture
but does not necessarily create more value for the supplier.

The model prevents “value” from meaning only revenue or customer delight. A
durable strategy considers whether customers, the organization, employees, and
suppliers have sufficient reason to continue participating.

The stick is a reasoning model, not a promise that every quantity can be
measured precisely. Its value lies in exposing whose value changed and whether
an apparent gain created value or merely moved it.
